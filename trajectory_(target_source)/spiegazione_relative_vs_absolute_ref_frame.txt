In Isaac Gym or Isaac Sim, when controlling a robot arm like the Franka Panda using inverse kinematics (IK),
the distinction between relative inverse kinematic control and absolute inverse kinematic control revolves 
around how target positions and orientations for the end-effector are defined and interpreted:

1. Relative Inverse Kinematic Control:

Definition: In relative IK control, the target position and orientation for the end-effector are specified
relative to its current position and orientation. This means the target is defined as an offset or change
from where the end-effector currently is.

Use Case: This is often used in scenarios where you want to incrementally move the arm in small steps, like 
moving a small distance forward, rotating slightly, or performing fine-tuned adjustments.

Example: If the end-effector is currently at position [x, y, z], and you want it to move forward by 
0.1 units along the X-axis, you specify a relative target of [0.1, 0, 0]. The IK solver would calculate 
the necessary joint movements to achieve this relative displacement.

Advantage: Provides more intuitive control for continuous or small incremental movements, as it’s based 
on the arm's current pose.


2. Absolute Inverse Kinematic Control:

Definition: In absolute IK control, the target position and orientation for the end-effector are specified 
in a fixed coordinate frame (usually the world or robot base frame). The target is defined as an absolute 
position and orientation in the environment.

Use Case: This is typically used when you need to move the end-effector to a specific point in space or to 
follow a predefined trajectory that is not relative to the current pose of the arm.

Example: If you want the end-effector to move to the position [x, y, z] = [0.5, 0.3, 0.2] in the world 
frame, you provide this absolute target, and the IK solver calculates the joint angles required to move 
the end-effector to exactly that point.

Advantage: Useful for precise positioning when the exact target position is known or needs to be achieved 
regardless of the current position of the arm.

Summary:

Relative IK: Moves the end-effector by a specified offset relative to its current pose.
Example: "Move 0.1 units forward from the current position."

Absolute IK: Moves the end-effector to a specific target location in the world or base frame.
Example: "Move to position [0.5, 0.3, 0.2] in the world frame."

In IsaacLab, depending on the task or control logic, you might choose relative IK for tasks that require 
continuous and small adjustments (e.g., tracking or following), while absolute IK is more suited for tasks 
that require precise and direct positioning.
